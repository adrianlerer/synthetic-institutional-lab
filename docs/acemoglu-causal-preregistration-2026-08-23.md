# Preregistration: Automation, Repression, and Institutional Lock-In

Date: 2026-08-23  
Status: protocol frozen; paid confirmatory run completed on 2026-08-23 under the separately authorized USD 10 hard cap  
Working paper title: **Automation, Repression, and Institutional Lock-In: A Causal Synthetic-Population Test of Political Adaptation to AI**

## 1. Single-Paper Contribution

This project will produce one integrated empirical paper. It combines:

- the automation, redistribution, revolt, and repression mechanism in Acemoglu, Gitmez, and Shadmehr (2026), *Automation and Repression*, NBER Working Paper 35336;
- the Dynamic Extended-Phenotype Causal Architecture (DEPCA), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22062929;
- TRIBE v2 as a controlled synthetic-population laboratory;
- a bounded institutional-digital-twin claim, available only after calibration and transport gates pass.

The paper does not treat LLM agents as people, nations, workers, voters, or valid replicas of Argentina. Its primary empirical object is model-system behavior under randomized institutional interventions.

## 2. Novelty Boundary

The paper must advance beyond:

1. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20405441, which reports model-specific phase transitions under constitutional lock-in.
2. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21876359, which defines a gated sim-to-real protocol for pre-legislative evidence.
3. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22062929, which supplies the causal architecture but reports no simulations or causal-effect estimates.

Publication requires intervention evidence about the automation-repression mechanism. A narrative application of the three prior papers is insufficient.

## 3. Causal Question

Under controlled institutional conditions, how do automation intensity, capital concentration, and the relative costs of redistribution and repression causally affect institutional response, worker coordination, welfare, and subsequent lock-in?

## 4. Variables and Time Order

- `M`: model family and immutable model/version metadata.
- `R`: provider and routing metadata.
- `L`: prompt language.
- `K_t`: capital concentration state.
- `A_t`: automation intensity.
- `S_t`: labor-share state communicated to the population.
- `Q_t`: revolt-coordination pressure.
- `C_redist`: marginal redistribution cost.
- `C_repress`: fixed repression cost.
- `D_t`: institutional response: regulate automation, redistribute, repress, adjudicate, or abstain.
- `H_t`: within-world institutional memory.
- `I_t`: coercive infrastructure accumulated after repression.
- `Lock_t`: institutional lock-in generated by accumulated infrastructure and prior decisions.
- `W_t`: welfare and constraint-satisfaction outcomes.
- `E_t`: measurement, parser, refusal, routing, or provider error.

Time-sliced graph:

`K_t, A_t -> S_t -> Q_t -> D_t`  
`C_redist, C_repress, Q_t -> D_t`  
`D_t -> I_(t+1), W_t`  
`I_t, H_t -> Lock_t -> D_t`  
`D_t, W_t -> H_(t+1)`  
`M, R, L -> D_t, E_t`  
`E_t -> observed(D_t)`

No same-cycle feedback arrow is permitted. Reciprocal causation is represented across cycles.

## 5. Confirmatory Treatments

The minimum confirmatory core uses eight prespecified cells rather than a full combinatorial grid.

| Cell | Automation | Capital concentration | Redistribution cost | Repression cost | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | low | low | low | high | redistribution-favoring baseline |
| C02 | high | low | low | high | automation effect at low capital |
| C03 | low | high | low | high | capital effect with costly repression |
| C04 | high | high | low | high | high automation/capital, redistribution viable |
| C05 | low | low | high | low | repression-favoring cost baseline |
| C06 | high | low | high | low | automation under cheap repression |
| C07 | low | high | high | low | capital under cheap repression |
| C08 | high | high | high | low | predicted strongest repression attractor |

English and stateful memory are confirmatory. Stateless memory and Spanish are separate robustness blocks and will not be pooled into the primary estimate.

An OpenAI control (`openai/gpt-5.4-mini`) was added before confirmatory analysis on 2026-08-23. It is reported as a separate control block and is not used to redefine the original three-model hypotheses after observing pilot outcomes.

## 6. Primary Estimands

All estimands use worlds, not events, as independent units.

1. Risk difference in repressive response under high versus low automation, averaged over the paired core cells.
2. Risk difference in repressive response under high versus low capital concentration.
3. Interaction between automation and capital concentration on repression.
4. Interaction between automation and the repression-favoring cost regime.
5. Effect of repression on next-cycle coercive infrastructure and institutional lock-in.
6. Indirect effect of automation through labor-share reduction and revolt pressure, reported only if mediator assumptions and measurements pass their gates.

Secondary outcomes include redistribution, regulation, adjudication, revolt participation, welfare, declared/action divergence, and palimpsest-layer use.

## 7. Hypotheses

- `H1`: high automation increases repressive institutional responses when the cost regime favors repression.
- `H2`: high capital concentration strengthens the automation-repression effect.
- `H3`: repression in one cycle increases coercive infrastructure and the probability of repression in later cycles, conditional on the same contemporaneous economic state.
- `H4`: redistribution attenuates revolt pressure but becomes less attractive in high-automation/high-capital cells when its marginal cost is high.
- `H5`: model family moderates effect size, but no difference will be described as national or cultural evidence.

## 8. Calibration Gate

Before any paid LLM run, a deterministic analytic fixture must pass these directional checks:

1. Holding costs and capital fixed, increasing automation cannot increase labor share.
2. Holding redistribution and repression fixed, lower labor share cannot decrease revolt pressure.
3. Holding automation and capital fixed, lowering repression cost cannot make repression less attractive in the analytic baseline.
4. Holding all current-period economic variables fixed, accumulated coercive infrastructure cannot reduce the lock-in score.

These are implementation checks, not empirical confirmation of the NBER model.

## 9. Treatment-Isolation Gate

For every paired cell, machine comparison must establish that:

- role definitions, base facts, output schema, model settings, and action vocabulary are identical;
- only the preregistered treatment fields and mechanically derived state values differ;
- filenames and prompts do not reveal expected outcomes or hypothesis labels;
- the model never sees `repression-favoring`, `baseline`, `predicted`, or cell-purpose text.

## 10. Measurement

The agent output schema will retain the existing TRIBE categories and add structured fields for:

- institutional policy choice;
- revolt participation choice where applicable;
- expected labor share and revolt pressure as bounded ordinal assessments;
- coercive-infrastructure reliance;
- cited normative layer.

Self-classification and deterministic judging remain separate measurements. The judge must be blinded to model and treatment cell.

## 11. Inference

- Primary summaries are world-level policy shares and world-level transitions.
- Uncertainty uses world-cluster bootstrap intervals.
- Event-level Fisher tests may be descriptive only and cannot supply the primary inferential claim.
- Missingness and parser/provider errors are reported by treatment, model, cycle, and role.
- Confirmatory, robustness, and exploratory results appear in separate tables.
- No mediation estimate is promoted if mediator-outcome confounding cannot be bounded credibly.

## 12. Planned Scale and Stop Rules

Provisional confirmatory scale:

- 8 cells;
- 3 reliable model lanes;
- 8 world replicas per cell and model;
- 3 cycles;
- 2 active decision roles per world, each assigned only to its relevant arm;
- capitalist state in `normative_reform`, worker coalition in `resource_allocation`.

Maximum planned calls: `8 × 3 × 8 × 3 × 2 = 1152`, before retries. The last factor is the two role-arm assignments; roles are not redundantly queried in both arms.

This scale is provisional until the live cost estimator and pilot variance calculation are complete. No paid run may start without:

- exact live model identifiers and prices;
- a hard `TRIBE_MAX_COST_USD` cap;
- a valid-action threshold of at least 95 percent after deterministic retries;
- explicit user approval of the displayed maximum cost.

## 13. Digital-Twin Claim Gate

Before external calibration, the artifact must be called a **causal synthetic institutional laboratory**.

The phrase **institutional digital twin** is allowed only if all of the following are satisfied:

- a named real institutional target is delimited;
- state variables are calibrated from provenance-bearing observations of that target;
- at least one out-of-sample trajectory is predicted before observation;
- calibration and predictive error are reported;
- the twin's update rule and validity horizon are explicit.

The phrase **digital twin of workers, voters, Argentina, or society** is blocked absent person-level and population-level validation that this protocol does not currently provide.

## 14. Publication Gate

The empirical paper remains `BLOCK` until the following artifacts exist:

- frozen prompts, cells, seeds, exclusions, estimands, and model/provider manifest;
- passing analytic calibration and treatment-isolation tests;
- user-approved paid-run receipt;
- immutable event and cost ledgers;
- world-cluster inference and missingness report;
- independent claim/evidence review;
- reproducibility archive tied to a public commit.

Until then, no expected result may be described as observed evidence.
